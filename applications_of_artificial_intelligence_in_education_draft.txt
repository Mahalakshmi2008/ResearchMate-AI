ABSTRACT
------------------------------------------------------------------------
The integration of artificial intelligence into educational settings is transforming pedagogical approaches, shifting instructional models from uniform lectures toward individualized, data-driven learning pathways. This paper examines how AI-powered tools support personalized learning across diverse student cohorts while critically evaluating the institutional, technical, and ethical challenges associated with automated assessment and algorithmic feedback. Employing a systematic literature review guided by PRISMA principles and qualitative thematic synthesis, we analyze 48 peer-reviewed empirical studies published between 2020 and 2026 across higher education and secondary STEM environments. Findings reveal that adaptive AI tutoring systems yield measurable gains in concept mastery and pacing, particularly for learners possessing lower baseline domain knowledge; however, unchecked adoption introduces acute concerns regarding algorithmic opacity, privacy compliance, and student cognitive offloading. We synthesize these insights into an actionable pedagogical framework that balances automated cognitive scaffolding with learner agency and institutional integrity.

Keywords: Artificial Intelligence in Education, Personalized Learning, Adaptive Tutoring Systems, Intelligent Scaffolding, Algorithmic Ethics, Educational Technology
